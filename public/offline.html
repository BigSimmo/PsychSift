Mental Health Act deadlines

Reference only — check against the Act and your service's procedure.

  • Detention of the voluntary inpatient so the assessment can be conducted

    Form 2

    “The voluntary inpatient can be detained under the order at the authorised hospital for up to 6 hours from the time when the order was made to enable the assessment to be conducted”

    Section 34 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Detention so the referred person can be taken to the authorised hospital or other place

    Form 3A

    “may make an order authorising the person’s detention for up to 24 hours from the time when the order is made”

    The Act also says (s 28): “The person cannot continue to be detained if the referral expires before the person is taken to the authorised hospital or other place”

    Section 28 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Longest continuous detention under section 28 orders, when the referral was made in a metropolitan area

    Form 3A

    “The person cannot be detained under orders made under this section for a continuous period of more than — (a)if the place where the referral is made is in a metropolitan area — 72 hours”

    The Act also says (s 28): “The person cannot continue to be detained if the referral expires before the person is taken to the authorised hospital or other place”

    Section 28 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Longest continuous detention under section 28 orders, when the referral was made outside a metropolitan area

    Form 3A

    “The person cannot be detained under orders made under this section for a continuous period of more than — … (b)if the place where the referral is made is outside a metropolitan area — 144 hours”

    The Act also says (s 28): “The person cannot continue to be detained if the referral expires before the person is taken to the authorised hospital or other place”

    Section 28 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Detention to take the person to the hospital, if a section 62 order is made

    Forms 3D, 6B

    Only if an order under section 62(1) is made to detain the person so they can be taken to the hospital

    “may make an order authorising the continuation of the person’s detention for up to 24 hours from the time when the order under section 61(1)(a) or (c) is made”

    Section 62 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Longest continuous detention, if a section 62 order is made

    Forms 3D, 6B

    Only if an order under section 62(1) is made to detain the person so they can be taken to the hospital

    “A person cannot be detained under orders made under this section for a continuous period of more than 72 hours”

    Section 62 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Confirmation of the community treatment order, if it was made under section 75

    Form 5A

    Section 76 · Signed off 26 September 2026

  • Advising the Chief Mental Health Advocate that a restriction on freedom of communication has been made

    Form 12C

    “must, within 24 hours after the time when the order is made, advise the Chief Mental Health Advocate that the order has been made”

    Section 262 · Signed off 26 September 2026
